# Contributing to cntryl projects

Thank you for contributing to a cntryl project. We welcome thoughtful
contributions from people using any development workflow, including AI-assisted
workflows. AI assistance is not a negative signal. We require transparency,
useful context, and contributor accountability for every submission.

Repository-specific instructions take precedence for project setup, validation,
architecture, and style. The expectations in this document apply throughout the
cntryl organization.

## Start with context

Explain why you are making the contribution:

- How did you encounter the problem or need?
- What project, workflow, or use case does it affect?
- What outcome do you expect?
- Why is the proposed scope appropriate for this repository?

Provide a minimal reproduction, concrete example, measurements, or other
supporting evidence when applicable. You do not need to disclose your legal
identity, private information, employer, or customer information.

Open an issue and wait for maintainer agreement before beginning a substantial
feature, architectural change, broad refactor, compatibility change, or other
large body of work. A small, self-contained correction may be submitted directly
when its motivation and evidence fit clearly in the pull request.

## Disclose material tool assistance

Disclose whether an AI or other generative tool materially assisted the issue,
pull request, code, documentation, tests, design, or analysis. When it did,
briefly describe:

- what kind of tool was used;
- which parts of the contribution it assisted; and
- how you reviewed and validated the resulting work.

Material assistance includes generating or substantially rewriting submitted
content or supplying analysis on which the contribution relies. Routine editor
completion, formatting, spelling correction, and search generally do not need
individual disclosure.

Do not include private prompts, credentials, proprietary source material, or
other confidential information in the disclosure.

## Take responsibility for the submission

By submitting a contribution, you are expected to:

- understand the complete change and explain its important behavior and design;
- review the full diff rather than forwarding generated output unexamined;
- run appropriate tests and report the exact validation performed;
- distinguish observed results from assumptions or unverified claims;
- respond to maintainer questions and make reasonable revisions; and
- have the right to submit the work under the repository's license.

The contributor remains responsible for the submission regardless of which
tools assisted it.

## Prepare issues and pull requests

Search existing issues and pull requests before opening a new one. Use the
provided form or template and complete every applicable section.

Keep pull requests focused. Link the accepted issue for substantial work,
describe what changed and why, identify compatibility or security risks, add or
update tests, and list the commands used for validation. Do not claim that a test
or check passed unless you ran it against the submitted change.

## Review and trust

Every contribution is evaluated on its technical merit, supporting evidence,
scope, contributor engagement, and project fit. AI disclosure does not count
against a contribution. Submission volume, prior trivial changes, polished
prose, account characteristics, or apparent authorship do not create an
entitlement to trust or merge.

Maintainers may close submissions that are duplicate, speculative, misleading,
mass-produced, unrelated to a demonstrated need, too broad without prior
agreement, missing required context or disclosure, or unsupported by a
contributor who cannot explain or revise the work. This protects limited review
capacity and the integrity of the project; it is not a judgment about a
contributor's identity or choice of tools.

Maintainers make the final decision about whether and when a contribution is
accepted.
